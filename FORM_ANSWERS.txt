# Evaluation Form Answers

**Brief summary of our approach:**
Our approach addresses the sequential matching setting, where participants arrive with incomplete information and hidden preferences. To manage the limited daily clarification budget, we strictly decouple information acquisition from match scoring. Rather than asking questions randomly or processing members in a simple queue, we allocate the budget using a potential-degree heuristic. This heuristic prioritizes querying the missing constraints of members who have the highest number of theoretically feasible partners, acting as a computationally efficient proxy for the usefulness of the requested information.

For the introduction phase, we rely on a greedy matching heuristic that prioritizes feasible pairs using an additive soft-field alignment. We deliberately favor this robust, static approach over complex probabilistic models or online learning algorithms. Because feedback on introductions is delayed by up to 7 days and the active decision horizon is strictly 60 days, any online learning process suffers from severe right-censoring near the episode boundary. Our experiments suggest that a stateless heuristic avoids the resulting variance and over-fitting, yielding more consistent matches.

**Key papers / references considered:**
Our approach is informed by three key works in the literature. We rely on Johari, Kamble, & Kanoria (2021), "Matching while learning," to contextualize the fundamental exploration-exploitation tradeoff when matching sequential arrivals under unknown preferences. Because the 60-day horizon makes systematic exploration extremely costly, we heavily prioritize exploitation. Furthermore, Joulani, Gyorgy & Szepesvári (2013), "Online learning under delayed feedback," provides theoretical background on the challenges introduced by delayed feedback. In our experiments, we observed high variance and overfitting under extreme delay and right-censoring, which motivated our architectural decision to abandon online learning in favor of robust static heuristics.

Finally, Krause & Guestrin (2009), "Optimal value of information in graphical models," serves as the general conceptual inspiration for our budgeted information acquisition and value-of-information reasoning. While we draw conceptually on their work to design our potential-degree heuristic, our heuristic is a problem-specific approximation. We explicitly note that our matching environment is not strictly submodular, and we do not claim that the cited paper theoretically guarantees our heuristic is optimal or submodular.

**Proposed method / algorithms:**
The proposed method operates as a two-phase daily policy. In the first phase, it calculates theoretical feasibility across the population to assign a "potential degree" to all constrained members. The daily clarification budget is then greedily allocated to members with the highest potential degree, prioritizing queries that are expected to have greater impact on the known feasible-pair graph. 

In the second phase, the policy constructs this feasible graph based strictly on known, unviolated hard constraints. It then extracts a greedy matching based on additive soft-field alignment. Crucially, the policy operates statelessly across days. Given the short 60-day decision horizon, delayed feedback, and the substantial opportunity cost of introductions, the policy forces immediate exploitation of known valid pairs rather than waiting or conducting exploratory matches.

**How will you evaluate your approach?**
We will evaluate our approach using the provided local Python simulator across all six scenario families. To combat the high stochasticity of date probabilities, we rely on a rigorous 10-seed evaluation generating 60 total episodes per variant. We compare our Final Hybrid policy against both the repository's Greedy baseline and a V4 policy variant that includes probabilistic expected-MSMI scoring and online learning, utilizing strict component ablations to isolate the impact of our potential-degree queries and static matching choices. The primary evaluation metric is the Mutual Second-Meeting Intention (MSMI) score, supported by secondary metrics including mutual acceptances, coverage, clarification cost, and the invalid/disqualification rate.

**Anything else reviewers should know?**
Our preliminary experimental evidence strongly supports the hypothesis that intelligent information acquisition paired with robust static matching can outperform naive queuing and high-variance learning in this setting. In our 10-seed evaluation, the final Hybrid policy achieved an overall MSMI of 0.392, an improvement over the Greedy baseline's 0.258 MSMI, all while generating zero invalid episodes. Most notably, the potential-degree heuristic provided strong empirical improvement in sparse network conditions, raising the cold_start scenario MSMI to 0.50. These empirical results provide preliminary evidence that prioritizing which information to acquire is a highly effective strategy for heavily constrained sequential matching environments.
